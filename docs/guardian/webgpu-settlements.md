# Experimental WebGPU settlements

`createWebGpuSettlements` consumes confirmed presentation state and resident native terrain. It loads existing culture-specific village geometry, center models and Bastion wall geometry and creates or removes presentation entries without changing simulation. A structure remains hidden until its terrain chunk arrives. Village transforms and wall morph/gate geometry reuse existing data; assets are shared within each loaded culture or material.

All five center cultures use baked vertex colors before their original textures load. Centers select BC7 KTX2 where supported and WebP otherwise. All five wall materials use validated baked linear RGB sidecars before loading the original atlas. Geometry is retained through hydration. Fine patterns are temporarily absent. The controlled BC7 test of a village, center and three walls retained five pipelines through texture transitions; separate WebP center tests created additional pipelines. These are functional checks, not evidence of reduced frametime or full visual parity. See `webgpu-progressive-all-structures.md` for package generation and coverage.

Center damage and collapse have a partial WebGPU presentation based on the native destruction kernel; repair restores the intact center. The interior, rotated normals, original ash material and destruction VFX remain incomplete, as described in `webgpu-center-destruction.md`. Lighting, shadows, streaming, interactions and all-culture visual parity require further validation. The production renderer remains unchanged.

The sidecars identify source UVs by SHA-256 without embedding buffer URLs, allowing production packaging to replace the source files with its shared wall buffer bank. The production build completed and all four zarzas UV ranges and texture identity were checked against packaged outputs; `tests/wall-buffer-bank.test.js` covers all five sidecars. This validates packaging, not shipped WebGPU runtime parity. `tools/experiments/audit_webgpu_settlements.mjs` exercises a WebGPU Worker with held center textures and wall atlas, transition, damage, repair and removal.

Village buildings also use a shared RGBA8 vertex-color sidecar, baked offline for each of the five cultures by `tools/experiments/bake_village_vertex_preview.mjs`. Four channels keep vertex stride aligned for WebGPU. The renderer validates the source geometry hash, sidecar hash, culture and layout, then shares one color attribute across the culture's existing draw ranges. It starts the atlas request independently, uploads it in the Worker and crossfades on the same geometry and material. A failed atlas leaves the provisional village visible; texture detail and visual parity are not claimed during that interval.

The held-atlas WebGPU test covers all three groups concurrently (village, center, walls), verifies that the village geometry and material identities remain unchanged after hydration and fails on uncaptured GPU errors. In the observed run there were four pipelines before and five afterwards: the additional pipeline was `mipmap-rgba8unorm-srgb`, created for texture preparation, not an observed material variant. A simulated 503 kept the village preview visible while center and wall transitions finished. In a new real game under the development flag, with the Worker atlas request held after the legacy scene loaded, all ten village units appeared with `villagePreviews=1` and `villageTextured=0`, then reached `villageTextured=1` after release. This confirms the incremental path, not an absence of long frames, original lighting parity or overall performance improvement. The sidecars total approximately 2.90 MB and the legacy renderer still runs underneath.
