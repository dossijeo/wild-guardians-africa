# WebGPU world integration in the game (development gate)

`?qa-worker-game&qa-webgpu-world` attaches an OffscreenCanvas WebGPU Worker to the live game's hosted simulation through ordered confirmed-state and native-terrain channels. The Worker rejects a non-WebGPU backend. The complete confirmed animal and worker rosters are enabled by default in this development mode; `qa-webgpu-single-actor` retains the narrower diagnostic. Camera poses are sent immediately with one update in flight and only the latest pending pose retained. A detected device failure removes the experimental canvas while the hosted simulation and old view remain available. This is not yet the production fallback: the legacy scene still runs underneath for interaction and reference, so these runs cannot demonstrate a performance benefit.

The experimental world includes native terrain and water, confirmed biome vegetation and solid props, original villages, centers, walls, animals, workers and crops. Geometries, materials and textures are shared where implemented; workers and animals retain independent CPU-evaluated skeletons. Animal physical poses come from the authority and are validated before application. Confirmed motion is validated and observed but does not drive meshes in GPU. Crop stages use the original baked V4 morph bridges when available; until their lazy-loaded asset is ready, the prior midpoint switch remains. The sky uses the original RGBE panoramas and confirmed simulation time, and Grow/Multiply confirmed spells and confirmed beast attacks have their original sprite layer. A natural raid produced 16 prepared attack sprites; Guardian Attack also has provisional WebGPU feedback reconciled by command ID; the animal provisional ragdoll and full visual parity remain pending. The effects still lack ribbons, rigid leaves, scene lights and soft depth; crop blessings, damage and all bridge visual comparisons remain incomplete.

All five animal species, village cultures, center cultures and wall materials have experimental vertex-color previews followed by their full textures. BC7 KTX2 is selected where supported; centers fall back to WebP. The provisional colors omit fine patterns until hydration. Controlled tests observed stable pipelines for BC7, while the center WebP fallback produced additional pipelines. These checks do not demonstrate the absence of long frames. Center damage uses the native deterministic destruction kernel with a TSL exterior and ash footprint; interior faces, rotated normals, original ash material, dust, fire and other VFX remain incomplete. These routes must not yet be considered production quality. The near-tree model is currently used at every distance. Terrain, water, lighting and materials remain provisional; environment reflections, animal shadows and complete visual parity remain incomplete. A TSL port of AfricanToon is connected to the animal bank and tracks confirmed lighting; its isolated buffalo comparison excludes shadows and reflections. A WebGPU roster test loaded 16 animals across three species, but the other species still need visual comparison.

`tools/experiments/audit_live_webgpu_world.mjs` checks the actual game in development mode. With `QA_RAID=1 QA_ALL=1 QA_PROPS=1 QA_WORKERS=1 QA_SETTLEMENTS=1 QA_CROPS=1 QA_NIGHT=1 QA_SKY=1`, an explicitly converted mangrove fixture produced 28 resident chunks, 143 crops with two active bridges, 23 workers, 11 animals, one center and 90 walls in WebGPU, with the original sky ready at night and a successful simulation pause. The fixture enables the island network and current world version for this diagnostic; it is not a saved-game compatibility or full-campaign test. A separate controlled device-loss run retained the hosted simulation; `audit_live_webgpu_physics.mjs` exercised one Rapier attack through fall, roll, recovery and release.

`QA_WEBGPU_WORLD=1 node tools/experiments/audit_worker_main_game.mjs` built a center, planted and hired through the normal UI in a new savanna game, then confirmed that the center, crop, worker and sky were ready in the WebGPU Worker. `QA_WEBGPU_WORLD=1 node tools/experiments/audit_worker_crop_provisional.mjs` verified the immediate provisional Grow action, its authoritative confirmation and one corresponding WebGPU agricultural effect. These are controlled functional tests, not visual parity, GPU timing, or performance comparisons.

The progression to production remains incomplete: migrate full materials and effects, autonomous trajectories and GPU skeletal animation; remove duplicate presentation; implement robust streaming and resource recovery; then validate gameplay and measure load time, CPU/GPU work and frame-time distributions on representative hardware. WebGL remains the compatibility route.

A separate opt-in `qa-webgpu-baked-hyena` exercises baked hyena walking in the Worker. The WebGPU shader compiled on an NVIDIA Turing adapter and was compared with the original rig at five phases, including shadows; differences remain in intermediate poses. It retains the original rig for physics and actions, follows confirmed simulation state, and is not autonomous animation. Real-raid and physical-transition validation is pending; the flag is off by default.

`QA_SPECIES=<species> node tools/experiments/audit_webgpu_progressive_real_character.mjs public/content/character-preview/<species>` checks each progressive model on WebGPU with textures held back; it changes a validated fixture entity only inside the visual Worker and is not a gameplay simulation. Hyena, lion, rhino and warthog all completed the BC7 transition without additional material pipelines in this controlled run. It does not measure first-appearance frame-time or prove visual parity.

The development-only `qa-webgpu-skip-legacy-draw` switch skips the duplicate legacy world draw while WebGPU is running, but retains legacy per-frame updates for interaction and falls back to the original draw if the Worker stops. A unit test covers selection and fallback; the attempted live device-loss audit could not complete because Vite stopped responding and the alternative server timed out preparing actors. The switch remains experimental and does not establish performance or a complete fallback.
