# WebGPU world integration in the game (development gate)

`?qa-worker-game&qa-webgpu-world&qa-webgpu-all-actors` attaches an OffscreenCanvas WebGPU Worker to the live game's hosted simulation through ordered confirmed-state and native-terrain channels. The Worker rejects a non-WebGPU backend. Camera poses are sent immediately with one update in flight and only the latest pending pose retained. A detected device failure removes the experimental canvas while the hosted simulation and old view remain available. This is not yet the production fallback: the legacy scene still runs underneath for interaction and reference, so these runs cannot demonstrate a performance benefit.

The experimental world includes native terrain and water, confirmed biome vegetation and solid props, original villages, centers, walls, animals, workers and crops. Geometries, materials and textures are shared where implemented; workers and animals retain independent CPU-evaluated skeletons. Animal physical poses come from the authority and are validated before application. Confirmed motion is validated and observed but does not drive meshes in GPU. Crop topology changes still lack the original morph bridge and blessing/VFX presentation. The near-tree model is currently used at every distance. Terrain, water, lighting and materials remain provisional; the original sky, AfricanToon, full effects and complete visual parity are not migrated.

All five center cultures and five wall materials have experimental vertex-color previews followed by their full textures. BC7 KTX2 is selected where supported; centers fall back to WebP. The provisional colors omit fine patterns until hydration. Controlled tests observed stable pipelines for BC7, while the center WebP fallback produced additional pipelines. These checks do not demonstrate the absence of long frames. Center damage uses the native deterministic destruction kernel with a TSL exterior and ash footprint; interior faces, rotated normals, original ash material, dust, fire and other VFX remain incomplete. These routes must not yet be considered production quality.

`tools/experiments/audit_live_webgpu_world.mjs` checks the actual game in development mode. With `QA_RAID=1 QA_ALL=1 QA_PROPS=1 QA_MOTION=1 QA_WORKERS=1 QA_SETTLEMENTS=1 QA_CROPS=1 QA_NIGHT=1`, an explicitly converted mangrove fixture produced 28 resident chunks, 143 crops, 23 workers, 11 animals, one center and 90 walls in WebGPU, with the expected night light intensities and a successful simulation pause. The fixture enables the island network and current world version for this diagnostic; it is not a saved-game compatibility or full-campaign test. A separate controlled device-loss run retained the hosted simulation; `audit_live_webgpu_physics.mjs` exercised one Rapier attack through fall, roll, recovery and release. The game-wide render, physics, save/restore, day/night visuals and performance still require comprehensive validation.

The progression to production remains incomplete: migrate the full shaders, sky, effects, morph transitions, autonomous trajectories and GPU skeletal animation; remove duplicate presentation; implement robust streaming and resource recovery; then validate gameplay and measure load time, CPU/GPU work and frame-time distributions on representative hardware. WebGL remains the compatibility route.
