# Buffalo facial authoring baseline

Active source: `public/assets/0c77a268cc0a7d22c66dedb7a471a66a414437f39e6bdfbc60c75dc36acf7920.glb`. No buffalo geometry or catalog entry has been edited yet.

The head and mouth directories contain four actual Blender inspection views each. Front and side mouth views were inspected. The lip seam is immediately below the muzzle, above the pointed beard; the beard tip and necklace teeth must not define the incision. No exposed oral teeth are visible in these closed-mouth views. Preserve the white muzzle markings and distinguish them from actual teeth. The collar teeth belong to the necklace and must remain independent of jaw opening.

`landmark-requests.json` records provisional image picks; `landmarks.json` raycasts those exact pixels onto the original mesh. The initial mouth seam samples are around world Blender Z 1.677–1.680 m, Y -0.460 to -0.383 m. These are initial anchors, not a complete incision curve. Refine the commissures against the mouth close-ups before cutting.

Topology differs substantially from the warthog: the imported mesh has 46,976 split vertices, while a disposable weld produces 7,889 vertices in one connected component. A whole-component jaw selection would therefore deform unrelated body and accessories. Author a local surface mask and inspect articulation/biconnected regions before assigning deformation. The production mesh was not welded.

Only 436 imported vertices exceed 0.7 Head weight, making the automatic head-weight inspection frame too tight (the `original` directory records this). The explicit head frame and mouth frame supersede it for anatomical inspection. Do not infer the complete facial skin weights from that count; inspect them before binding lids or jaw.

The inspection helper now resolves its output directory absolutely before assigning Blender render paths, preventing relative render paths from escaping the requested workspace directory. All images in this report are copied/saved in the repository.

## Skinning constraint discovered

`weights.json`, generated by `inspect_facial_weights.py` using the explicit boxes in `weight-regions.json`, shows that the muzzle is mostly Neck-weighted (mean 0.678 Neck / 0.321 Head), and the lower lip/beard even more so (0.801 Neck / 0.198 Head). The eye-region box mixes Head and Neck (0.589 / 0.407). These boxes include adjacent surface, so their averages are not per-eyeball assignments.

The disposable biconnected decomposition also returns one large 7,889-vertex part; unlike the warthog tusk, this mesh cannot be separated by removing one articulation vertex. A broad Head-only jaw or lid binding would disagree with the existing moving surface. Preserve original weights on surviving vertices and transfer local surface weights to new oral geometry. For eyelids, either match local source weights or explicitly author and validate a tightly bounded skinning correction; never rigidify the whole muzzle or necklace by implication. This finding changes the authoring approach and must be tested in Running and attack poses, not only rest pose.

## Eyelid candidates v1 / v2 (not accepted)

The new opt-in `surfaceSkinning` profile mode in `add_actor_eyelids.py` transfers barycentric source weights from each lid vertex's fully closed surface position, retains the four strongest normalized influences, and leaves original skin weights untouched. Other existing profiles keep their previous behavior. The buffalo profile uses this mode and the explicit eye inspection frame.

Both candidates were generated and exported with original animation channels restored. Each passes the 61-bone, six-clip, 246-pose audit (zero position error; maximum angular error 7.30e-8 rad). Authoring reports record zero original skin changes and the transferred binding counts. These checks prove channel preservation, not animated lid clearance.

Visual inspection rejected v1: full closure leaves white eyeball exposed laterally. V2 extends the exterior and lower contours; it reduces that exposed area but introduces an undesirable curled fold and still leaves a small edge visible in profile. The `lids-v1` and `lids-v2` renders retain those failures. Neither is installed. Next correction must address the curved eye envelope, not simply continue extending a front-projected patch. Neutral and intermediate blink poses and moving-pose skinning remain unvalidated.

## Curved eyelid iteration v3 / v4

The authoring helper now accepts explicitly reviewed `eyeSurfaceSamples` for eyes that cannot be separated as connected components. It raycasts those pixels, fits a sphere to their actual positions, bounds its radius and uses the existing quarter-step spherical lid construction. Surface-skinning transfer samples the resulting closed sphere rather than the old projected patch. Future reports retain the complete profile for reproduction.

V3 removed the curled fold in the inspected side view, but missed a small interior edge on the left eye. V4 adds visible-eye samples at that corner; the front and opposite profile were inspected and no exposed iris/white edge was apparent at full closure. This is static full-closure evidence only. Original animations still pass the 61-bone, 246-pose audit. Neutral shape, partial blinks and source-weight compatibility in animated poses must be checked before acceptance. The active buffalo is unchanged; current editable candidate is `C:/tmp/guardian-faces/buffalo-lids-v4.blend`.

## Jaw incision v1 — diagnostic, not installed

`prototype_buffalo_jaw.py` uses the raycast side-view lip landmarks to cut a piecewise curved seam, rather than a horizontal plane through the beard. Original surface skinning is retained; new cut vertices interpolate it. Only the lower muzzle and beard receive the tapered jaw morph. Source animation channels are restored after Blender export.

The front and side full-opening renders in `jaw-v1` were visually inspected. The opening follows the lower muzzle boundary and the necklace shows no obvious drag in these static views. However, the side view exposes a bright inner edge, and no palate, floor, tongue or recessed oral walls have yet been authored. The dark front opening is not evidence of a completed interior. Do not install this candidate or treat the renders as a passed mouth validation.

`jaw-v1-animation-audit.json` verifies 61 bones across six clips and 246 poses: zero positional difference and maximum angular difference 7.30e-8 radians. This verifies the original animation trajectories only, not deformed-surface clearance. Next: model the actual recessed interior, correct the exposed rim, inspect neutral/intermediate/full opening from both profiles, and test the inherited skinning in running and attack poses.

## Oral cavity v1 — not installed

`add_buffalo_oral_cavity.py` orders the 55 incision landmarks as one open contour and builds a recessed palate, lower floor, rear wall, commissure walls and rounded tongue. It leaves the front aperture open. The 1,198 new vertices / 1,128 faces use one tissue material and barycentrically interpolated source lip weights (four normalized influences), rather than rigid Head binding. The lower surfaces share the jaw rotation with depth taper.

Executed Blender renders cover neutral, 50% and full mouth opening from four views. Inspected front and side at full opening show a volumetric interior and no former white profile rim; the half-opening front retains depth. The neutral front reveals an overly exposed reddish line under the muzzle. This is an unresolved quality defect: recess the oral boundary and repeat closure checks before installing. Animated skinning, both-profile intermediate clearance and runtime expression coupling remain unverified.

The six original clips again pass the 246-pose channel audit with zero positional error and 7.30e-8 rad maximum angular error. The candidate is `C:/tmp/guardian-faces/buffalo-oral-v1.blend`; the reproducible authoring script, geometry report and rendered evidence are committed. No production buffalo asset was replaced.

## Oral cavity v2 and first Three.js pose check

Inset the cavity boundary 2.5% toward its recessed rear, removing the exposed red line in the inspected neutral frontal Blender render. The fully open side retains the interior without the previous white rim. Neutral/open four-view render sets and the unchanged six-clip channel audit are retained.

In the actual Three.js expression fixture, Running at 0.35 s with mouth 0.85 and both eyes closed binds both Mesh_0 and BuffaloOralAnatomy, plus eight eyelid corrective channels. Visual limitation discovered: the oral floor appears too uniformly pink/flat under the runtime hemisphere and directional lighting, unlike the shaded Blender render. This is not accepted final material quality. Investigate interior normals, tissue shading and recessed ambient occlusion without covering the aperture. Animated attachment is sampled in only this single Running pose; attack, neutral running and full clip traversal still require validation. Active asset catalog remains unchanged.

Corrected tongue winding and explicitly oriented palate/floor faces toward the cavity. Candidate source is `C:/tmp/guardian-faces/buffalo-oral-v4.blend`.

## Source comparison resolves the beard regression question

The fixture now accepts `?actor=buffalo&original=1` to load the exact original asset without overwriting the candidate. `source-attack-comparison/original.png` and its runtime state show Charged_Upward_Slash at 0.6 s, neutral morphs, three-quarter face view. The original also has the pointed/jagged beard silhouette observed in the edited candidate. Screenshots use different viewport sizes, so this is a pose comparison rather than pixel-difference evidence.

A new opt-in `--buffalo-beard` gate in `audit_actor_face.mjs` matches retained neutral beard vertices by rest position and compares their skinned world positions over all 246 sampled poses in six clips. For oral v4, 594 edited vertices match original beard vertices, with maximum motion difference 1.873e-7 m. Thus the observed neutral beard contour is inherited, not newly introduced motion in those retained vertices. Preserve its authored pointed shape instead of smoothing it on the assumption of a regression.

This test does not validate newly inserted lip vertices, open-mouth anatomy, or all source surface topology. The remaining review concerns are runtime interior appearance and expression clearance through moving poses; the candidate is still not installed.
