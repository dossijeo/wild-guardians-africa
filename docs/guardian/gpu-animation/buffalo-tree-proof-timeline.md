# Buffalo camera: tree preparation and fade

The close buffalo camera exposes an avoidable delay after a discontinuous QA camera move in the native Wild Engine island mangroves. The readiness value alone is not proof that a 3D tree is actually drawn. This audit separates the transition controller's verified target from its current blend value, and records both every frame. The frustum-sphere selection is conservative; it does not establish pixel visibility or measure occlusion.

On the GTX 1660 using headless Edge, all 31 selected trees within 90 horizontal units already had a target of 1 in the first captured frame, including a tree 25.16 units away. In the one-second configuration, all 31 were still blending at frame 25 and reached 1 at frame 30. In a separate 0.1-second diagnostic run, all 31 reached 1 at frame 3. The first frame was already partially advanced by earlier setup renders. The test uses a fixed visual delta of 1/30 s after the camera teleport; frame counts are not a wall-clock latency measurement. Reports are `buffalo-tree-proof-timeline/report.json` and `buffalo-tree-proof-timeline-0.1/report.json`.

Three possible approaches remain to compare: prepare and advance the incoming camera's coverage before revealing it; shorten or eliminate the fade only for genuinely close trees after GPU readiness; or retain the current gradual transition and improve camera anticipation. The first can remove visible replacement during a planned transition, but cannot by itself solve arbitrary camera jumps. The second can produce an obvious simultaneous pop and a GPU spike. The third still exposes impostors when preparation or anticipation loses the race. None is accepted for production by these observations alone.

The 0.1-second run shows a shorter exposure, not equivalence: it has not established that the replacement is visually unobjectionable in motion or across biomes and camera paths. Capture sequences, frame pacing and GPU timers under normal gameplay, camera transitions and slow resource preparation before changing the default. The reported render submission durations include this diagnostic setup and are not representative of game-wide CPU or GPU performance.

## Prepared handoff experiment

The loading cinematic already advances verified targets before the world is revealed; the buffalo harness instead teleports its camera without such a handoff. A separate QA experiment advances only targets already covered by the transition controller's GPU-readiness proof. In this camera, all 31 selected trees report full readiness on the first capture and remain so through frame 120. The test then performs a full `World.render(0, 0)` to update packing and draw the prepared state. See `buffalo-tree-prepared-snap-full-render/report.json` and `frame-0.png`. This is still a visible-camera diagnostic with an initial draw before the captured draw, not proof that a hidden handoff occurred or that arbitrary camera movements are solved.

A rejected intermediate experiment advanced the transition, called only `farVegetation.update(0)`, then drew the world directly. Its first image, `buffalo-tree-prepared-snap/frame-0.png`, has missing 3D trunks despite reporting readiness 1; the next ordinary frame restores them. The full render's first image shows the trunks. This demonstrates that the readiness flag is not sufficient to prove draw packing is current. Do not copy the rejected direct-draw sequence into production. Neither experiment measures an uninstrumented frame or GPU cost; slow GPU preparation, actual loading transitions, movement and other biomes remain unverified.

## Actual loading probe

`tools/trees/audit_foreground_tree_live_reveal.mjs` now accepts `QA_SPATIAL_FAR=1` to explicitly activate the spatial far layer in the real application and rejects a run without nearby observed trees after handoff. Without this opt-in, the application currently disables this layer when a spatial session is present unless the `spatialFar=1` URL parameter is supplied; an empty adapter list cannot validate its handoff. The audit also waits for the menu iframe to navigate before dispatching the start event.

On seed 712 in native island mangroves, with the opt-in enabled and the existing loading cinematic unchanged, the first post-handoff observation found 12 of 12 sampled trees within 80 horizontal units at readiness 1; subsequent observations in this run likewise found no sampled unready trees. The image `buffalo-tree-real-loading-spatial-far.png` was captured approximately 1.8 seconds after handoff. The detailed report is `../../lighting/tree-near-retexture/performance/mangrove-foreground-live-reveal-actual-spatial-far-probe.json`. Observations occur at most every 250 ms, use conservative physical/frustum checks and are not per-pixel proof. They cannot exclude a missing or impostor-only frame between samples, and this single startup and camera do not validate gameplay camera jumps or other biomes. Recorded frame intervals include loading, diagnostics and audio, and are not an uncontaminated A/B performance comparison.

With `QA_PER_FRAME_TREES=1`, a separate instrumented run sampled every world update in the first second after handoff and retained four framebuffer images at approximately 0, 0.5, 1 and 1.5 seconds. It recorded 28 updates from 13 to 971 ms; only six contained nearby trees because the camera was initially looking at the sky, and none of their selected trees reported readiness below 1. The images at 1046 and 1518 ms show the forest as the camera lowers (`buffalo-tree-real-reveal-1s.png` and `buffalo-tree-real-reveal-1.5s.png`). The full report is `../../lighting/tree-near-retexture/performance/mangrove-foreground-live-reveal-actual-spatial-far-reveal-sequence.json`. These are four images, not an uninterrupted pixel comparison; per-frame state still does not prove the corresponding geometry was drawn. Readback, checks and sampling perturb timings, so this run cannot measure performance.

## Moving camera baseline

The actual application was run with `QA_SPATIAL_FAR=1 QA_CAMERA_PAUSE=1 QA_CAMERA_PENDING=1 QA_CAMERA_SMOOTH=1 RUN_SUFFIX=camera-pending-smooth-spatial-far`: the simulation was paused and the camera moved for four seconds toward a tree whose initial readiness was zero. The camera moved approximately 196 horizontal units, a deliberately fast stress case, not a measured typical player path. In samples taken approximately every 250 ms, a sample at 1382 ms contained two transitioning trees within 80 units (nearest 76.4, blend 0.67); at 1633 ms it contained ten (nearest 65.0, blend 0.93). Their transition targets were 1 and the prepared standby representation was available, although their native batch was not yet marked prepared. Later samples included a few additional transitioning trees, and the chosen target was ready when first observed within the 80-unit sample radius. The detailed `cameraPause` trace is in `../../lighting/tree-near-retexture/performance/mangrove-foreground-live-reveal-camera-pending-smooth-spatial-far.json`.

This demonstrates that a smooth camera move can encounter partial billboard coverage after the 3D standby is available. It does not prove those trees were unobstructed on screen, determine the exact first or last visible frame, or demonstrate a GPU cost or improvement. The report is instrumented and includes startup, so its overall frame percentiles must not be used as an A/B benchmark. Compare visual continuity and complete CPU/GPU/frame costs of anticipation, a shorter near-only fade, and the unchanged path before accepting a production change; preserve the fallback when no prepared geometry exists.

An independent exploratory run of the same scripted path used `QA_TREE_FADE_SECONDS=0.1`, which intercepts the transition module only in the test browser and leaves GPU-ready requirements intact. Both runs selected `spatial:0:-1:21` and yielded 29 camera samples. The one-second baseline had four samples with partially transitioned trees (15 tree observations, nearest 65 units); the 0.1-second run had none. See `../../lighting/tree-near-retexture/performance/mangrove-foreground-live-reveal-camera-pending-smooth-spatial-far-fade01.json`. The samples are roughly 250 ms apart, longer than the experimental fade itself: missing a transition is expected, not evidence that it became imperceptible. Different runs also have different preparation timing. This is not a controlled visual or performance A/B, and a global 0.1-second fade is not accepted for production.
