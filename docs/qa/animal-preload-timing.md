# First appearance: model preparation and remaining camera cost

The optional `timing=1` mode in [the native fixture](../../tests/browser/animal-preload.html) records CPU duration of render calls, RAF intervals, asynchronous rig acquisition and the CPU time inside `NativeHorizon.update`. It pays for a normal centre and spawns the five actual species. It does not advance the simulation, drive workers, measure GPU time or emulate phone hardware. Rig acquisition includes promise scheduling; it must not be read as exclusive CPU cost per model. Camera focus and real terrain streaming remain part of the appearance.

[The initial sample](animal-preload-timing/gran-canon.json) contains an unsettled baseline with queued terrain work; it is preserved as exploratory evidence, not an idle comparison. Later samples first drain that work and capture 24 baseline frames and 60 frames after appearance. RAF intervals also include browser scheduling and concurrent machine activity; no universal FPS threshold is asserted.

[Before retaining the horizon material](animal-preload-timing/gran-canon-settled.json), all five animals reused their prepared rigs, seven GLB downloads remained seven, and no new animal shader programs appeared. A new program did appear on a `MeshStandardMaterial` under `native-horizon`. The first render call took 402.6 ms with zero animal rigs attached; the second took 28.9 ms with all five attached. This contradicts attributing the entire spike to loading an animal model.

`NativeHorizon` previously disposed its only terrain material whenever the camera changed resident regions. Three released that material's program, making the next horizon draw compile it again. The horizon now retains the material across compatible region changes, updates the same clipping-bound vector and replaces only geometry. Changing biome/configuration or material quality still releases it; final world disposal also releases it. It does not add a second material or geometry cache.

[After retaining the material](animal-preload-timing/gran-canon-retained-material.json), the tested appearance introduced no new programs of any kind. These single samples are not a controlled estimate of milliseconds saved. [A further instrumented sample](animal-preload-timing/gran-canon-horizon-cost.json) still spent 189.9 ms inside horizon reconstruction out of a 205.7 ms first render, before any animal rig was attached. The second render was 21 ms. Asynchronous geometry generation or incremental horizon construction remains necessary to address that remaining camera spike; the model preload must not be described as eliminating all incursion hitches.

The affected horizon, rendering-quality, animal-preload, cold-actor and world-disposal suites pass together: 21 cases, zero failures or skips. Production build and web-package validation also pass. These checks cover material lifetime, clipping bounds, quality/configuration changes, renderer cancellation and resource ownership; they do not replace visual mobile verification.

[Desert](animal-preload-timing/desierto-retained-material.json) also retains seven downloads and introduces no new programs. Its first render spends 290.6 ms inside horizon reconstruction out of 299.7 ms; the second is 13.5 ms with five rigs attached. Raid spawning itself takes 354.8 ms in that sample and is reported separately. Navigation/spawn planning and horizon geometry remain independent optimization targets. The reports after the change use this working tree's horizon implementation; the historical reports retain the earlier implementation and evolving diagnostic fixture. No report is presented as a full campaign on one immutable HEAD.
