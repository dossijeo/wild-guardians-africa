# Structural follow-up, without changing the candidate

The camera-independent audit reads the original raw GLB, authoritative source face labels and immutable `.5` forward archive. It coalesces only exactly equal Float32 geometric positions (signed zero canonicalized), keeping UV/normal attributes untouched. It diagnoses adjacency, coincident cross-label boundaries, winding versus stored normals, zero area and finite values. Reproduce with `node tools/frontside_shared_leaf_structural_audit.mjs`. It does not change geometry, select faces from pictures, recalculate normals or cap boundaries.

Both original and derived forward corners are finite, with no zero-normal corners, zero-area triangles or face normals opposing their stored corner normals. None of the nine entire labelled regions is a closed consistently oriented component under this exact-edge diagnostic. Many original region boundaries coincide with another label: e.g. all72 boundary edges of label8 and144/146 for label6. They can be internal interfaces; treating every labelled region as an isolated open leaf or filling those loops would be unsupported. The forward derivative has substantially more exact boundary edges, so the audit cannot certify continuous welded interfaces after reduction. Near-coincident edges and T-junctions are not resolved by this exact test. Signed volumes of those open regions are origin-dependent diagnostics, not an inward/outward classification.

The pilot's labels>=2 selector denotes botanical regions and remains an explicit experimental bilateral treatment, not proof that every organ is a planar leaf. Reverses are shared indices and preserve the source back-facing shader convention, while core geometry is unchanged. The current candidate and its native captures are not altered by these findings. Quality still needs the policy3 appearance/continuous-growth review; there is no automatic topology or pixel rejection. Before extending other states/species, identify true bilateral surfaces and closed/interface components independently of cameras rather than blindly applying the same selector everywhere.
