# Village atlas and normal experiment (experimental, rejected)

The original village meshes and atlases remain untouched. This compares Mapungubwe's first building and Musgum's first building in the real WebGL scene (case 1 and 3, seed 712, fixed camera, day/night). `visualLab=0` is the reference. `normal-relief` uses the existing authored normal map and a small continuous directional-light contribution inside the illustrated material. `normal-enhanced` additionally uses a Blender-derived normal atlas. `texture-detail` tests a same-resolution warm-surface micrograin color atlas. None is enabled by default.

## Audit

- Each village is a compact interleaved position/normal/UV binary with a 2048² color, normal and roughness atlas. The unit mesh uses one shared material; there is no material ID or semantic mask for paja, madera, barro or trim. `tools/prepare_content.py` refers to `Poblados_Lab_V5_Mapungubwe_Saheliano_Suajili_Musgum_Etiope`, but that source directory and original village GLBs are absent from this checkout. We therefore inspected the runtime binary and atlases, without claiming a source-GLB comparison.
- Original geometric normals are unit length (all five cultures, min/max round to 1.000), and UVs span the atlas. The authored normal maps have RGB XY standard deviation of ~23–30/255, so they are not flat. At least Mapungubwe and Musgum contain detailed normal information.
- Median world-space texel densities of the first three Mapungubwe units are 27.6, 36.7 and 34.5 texels/m; Musgum: 38.9, 48.0 and 40.1 texels/m. Run `python tools/experiments/audit-village-atlases.py` for the reproducible per-unit calculation.
- At native 1:1 pixels, the color atlases retain painted cracks, roof seams and trim. Some large color areas are intentionally smooth. The atlas is fragmented into many small UV islands. `Assets.texture()` uses ordinary mipmapped linear filtering and no special anisotropy. The village material loads neither the authored roughness atlas nor a normal atlas in the production path. Its illustrated `artLighting416` quantizes broad light bands and replaces the standard material's PBR result; this is the principal reason a normal map has little impact at gameplay distance.

## Results

The `normal-relief` pilot affects 6.95% of case-1 close-view pixels by >12/255 by day and 4.87% by night. At the full camera the mean absolute RGB change is only ~0.01/255. Musgum's close view changes more (16.56% by day), but gains hard mottled patches on mud. The enhanced Blender atlas exaggerates those patches. Neither improves the normal gameplay view enough to justify the visual regression. `texture-detail` changes <0.01/255 mean RGB at full camera while growing the Mapungubwe download from 1,279,936 to 1,565,184 bytes and Musgum from 1,316,700 to 1,621,672 bytes. Anisotropic filtering at 8× also produced almost no visible change and is not selected.

ABBA measurements with Chrome hardware WebGL at 1280×720 are in `abba-relief.json`, `abba-enhanced.json` and `abba-color-detail.json`. The two-sample GPU means vary roughly ±1 ms across cases and order; there is no repeatable total-frame benefit. For `normal-relief`, case-1 day GPU was 17.54→17.54 ms and case-3 night 19.39→20.00 ms; CPU submission was within ~0.3 ms. Draw calls and triangles were unchanged. The stronger normal map adds a 2048² RGBA atlas (about 21.3 MiB with mipmaps) per loaded culture versus the production path; its downloads are 1.59 MB and 1.88 MB. The color-detail candidate replaces the existing texture at the same 2048² size and thus needs no additional decoded texture, but increases download size by ~0.29–0.31 MB per culture. These estimates concern texture storage, not measured process RSS.

Compare the actual scene captures at `case-01-0-close-day.webp`, `case-01-normal-relief-close-day.webp`, `case-03-0-close-night.webp`, and `case-03-normal-enhanced-close-night.webp`. Corresponding `*-full-*` files show the gameplay camera. Native-resolution atlas crops are in `../village-texture-audit/`. No external assets were used. The Blender scripts `tools/experiments/build-village-normal-relief.py` and `build-village-color-detail.py` preserve source dimensions and colors/UVs while producing isolated WebP variants. These trials show that uniformly intensifying a packed atlas cannot isolate roof straw from painted trim or mud and can harm legibility.

Recommendation: keep the original renderer. A future quality gain requires a per-surface material mask or source-level reauthoring for one model, not global normal amplification or uniform grain. That next isolated experiment is recorded separately.
