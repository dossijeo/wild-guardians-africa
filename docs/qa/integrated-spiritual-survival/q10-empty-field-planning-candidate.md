# Q10 empty-field labour planning candidate — not integrated

The passive empalizada seed-712 campaign's dawn defeat after night nine exposed a QA policy failure: after harvesting the whole field, Q9 renewed only one worker, then bought 37 new seedlings. That crew generated no further delivered income before reserves ran out. This must not be interpreted as evidence that stronger walls or passive agriculture are inherently economically invalid.

The standalone `tools/native-q10-labour-policy.mjs` candidate retains Q9 for day one and any nonempty field. When an operational field is empty, it forecasts how many seedlings current cash can fund after actual damaged-structure repair quotes and two daily wage reserves. It selects a funded crew using the existing six-work-units-per-renewed-worker heuristic. Quotes use native proportional repair pricing, individually rounded up. Overlapping pending repairs are reserved once via the maximum, not added twice.

Example planning fixtures: with 332 coins and no repair quote it selects four workers at 30 coins, leaving a next-day wage reserve and an eighteen-seedling forecast. With a 70-coin native reconstruction quote it selects three workers and forecasts sixteen seedlings. Forecasts are planning inputs, never granted plants, income, synthetic actions or crop quotas. Later actual delivered income can fund additional planting. Low-capital cases retain the original plan with an explicit insufficient-recovery diagnostic. The candidate does not yet address the separate nonempty-field over-renewal risk.

Four unit tests cover state immutability, exact native damage quotes, first-day/nonempty/insufficient-capital behaviour and scaling without a crop quota. These synthetic fixtures establish planning invariants only. No campaign imports Q10 yet; live campaigns remain frozen on Q9. Native seven/fourteen-night comparison must follow integration before claiming an economic improvement. Agricultural prices, workers, productivity, magic, physical damage and salary settlement are unchanged.
