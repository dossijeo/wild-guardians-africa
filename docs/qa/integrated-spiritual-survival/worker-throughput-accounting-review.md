# Native worker contract and delivery diagnostics

`audit-native-worker-throughput.mjs` reconciles each recorded hire to its original profile and native proportional wage, then to the actual salary ledger movement. Every delivered crate must have a unique identifier, worker identifier and exact matching delivery payment. Daily wage and income subtotals must reconcile. Paid contract seconds are calculated from actual hire time and the original profile's shift end; this is contract capacity, not measured working time. Discrete busy/active samples remain separate from human activity or continuous worker utilization.

The completed Q10 passive pilot passes these checks. On days eight through ten, three 30-coin workers each provide 900 paid contract-seconds per day. Real deliveries are 14 / 17 / 22, yielding 154 / 187 / 242 coins. Wages per delivered crate are approximately 6.43 / 5.29 / 4.09 coins. Actual net daily cashflow after seeds and repairs is -196 / -14 / +9. On days nine and ten all workers are busy in all 29 sampled observations. These ratios describe cashflow, not causal profit: a delivery may originate from an earlier crop or contract cycle. There is no basis to count the full 900 seconds as useful work or human activity.

Three tests cover full/proportional native hiring, distinct profiles, exact delivery/ledger matching and rejection of inconsistent wages, duplicate crates, missing payments or impossible busy counts. The initial fixture at exactly second 100 exposed a real boundary problem rather than a throughput calculation failure: a 30-coin worker with a 300-second shift should cost 20 at that point, but the existing native `ceil(wage*(1-time/end))` calculation returns 21 due to floating-point subtraction. The command-time fixture was moved to an exact half shift to validate observed accounting without codifying the incorrect boundary as a required behaviour.

`proportional-wage-boundary-diagnostic.json` records the read-only integer-time comparison and workforce source hash. Correct the boundary calculation after frozen campaigns finish, preserve their existing paid charges and repeat the relevant hire/ledger tests. Do not silently recompute historical campaign wages. This small defect does not explain the sustained passive insolvency and is not a reason to change the original 30/40 wages.
