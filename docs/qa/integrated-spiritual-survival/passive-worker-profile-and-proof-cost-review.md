Experimental integration branch only; no main integration or campaign acceptance.

## Seven-night profile control

Both v94 and v95 terminated normally after seven observed nights. All frozen source hashes are identical. The recorded input guard verifies that worker profile is the only player-option difference, with six opening workers in both cases. Both use q9, cashflow planting, rolling zarzas funding from day six, seed 712 and Savanna/Mapungubwe. Prices and physical raid rules are unchanged.

Neither proposed perimeter closed. These are paid purchases and real attacks, not hypothetical protection. The young-worker policy did not improve cash reserves or defensive completion sufficiently to justify a long extension. This is a policy outcome, not proof that young workers are intrinsically unprofitable or that passive play cannot survive.

Each terminal observation passed ledger/delivery/raid conservation, plant census, paid-worker throughput, defense allocation, defense costs and magic-delivery audits. Agricultural magic income is zero in both passive controls; defensive shielding remains enabled. The v94 seven-day full rows exactly reproduce v77. The explicit replay audit permits only the boundary graph implementation change and verifies both historical/reference and candidate hashes; it does not weaken the usual identical-source comparison gate.

The reported decision-opportunity inactivity proxies are about 76.3% and 76.9%. They do not measure human action duration or establish the requested manual inactivity threshold.

## Independent hypothetical-perimeter diagnosis

On the retained, cooperatively stopped v90 Canyon snapshot, the read-only native hypothetical-perimeter proof took 24,717.96 ms with CPU profiling enabled and 10,536.23 ms on a subsequent warmed query. Both performed 14,563 approach-path calls and five group-blockage queries. Walkability calls decreased from 317,387 to 152,339. The first duration includes instrumentation and cannot be compared as an optimization effect against the warmed duration.

The first diagnostic version asserted complete object equality and failed because closed-region Set identity counts merge as the cache warms. Its exact tool and raw CPU profile are retained. Version two preserves both complete outputs, compares physical conclusions and all other diagnostics, explicitly excluding only the `regions` counter, and asserts unchanged serialized state. That comparison passes. No navigation result is mocked and unpaid hypothetical walls are not real protection.

The profiles identify repeated terrain and native navigation work inside the QA service-position proof. This may explain campaign wall-planning CPU cost; it is not evidence of GPU cost, gameplay stutter, enemy inactivity or economic defeat. Any subsequent optimization must preserve physical reachability checks and retain these original profiles. Live campaign code remains frozen.
