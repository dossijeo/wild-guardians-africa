# Exact day-thirteen checkpoint and bounded night replay

`pilot-v123-day13-preraid-checkpoint/` repeats the frozen native Sabana / Mapungubwe / seed 123 Q15 good campaign under commit `f9806d01`. Its first twelve daily JSON rows are byte-for-byte identical to v122. The new diagnostic-only CLI option copied the exact state after the tick reaching day 13, time 295 s, then stopped cooperatively. It neither altered the virtual player's decisions nor advanced the campaign past that point. The checkpoint SHA-256 is `3984DDC1C78C9E219D45DA147F30AB0C6F81D749B57AC647B0C5BF3396DD5B69`.

`tools/profile-native-night-entry-checkpoint.mjs` reconstructs the native navigator and entry worker from that copied state and advances with real `Game.tick` calls but **without** resuming player decisions. This is a CPU diagnostic, not an economic or military campaign. The segment to time 360 s cost 1.37 s in native ticks and had no active raid. Extending to time 390 s cost 12.29 s in ticks; 10.91 s occurred in the single 5-s tick beginning at time 385 s, where the sixteen-animal raid spawned. This first spawn tick is expensive, but its measured cost does not by itself explain the multi-minute full-campaign slowdown. The late time-390 raid snapshot likewise advanced twenty simulated seconds in about 1.74 s in a separate isolated replay. Player policy and repeated geometry validation during the remaining day-thirteen decisions require direct timing.

The same checkpoint's retained paid contour has six missing wall slots and zero legal new pieces. A full native proof took 2.60 s on the first call, 3.78 ms on an identical repeat, and returned `native-worker-route-blocked`. This confirms the positive service cache works at day-thirteen density, while showing that a changed predictive view still pays the first-call cost. Per-step decision and tick timing is therefore the next diagnostic; it is not evidence to change damage, prices, labor, magic or the frozen player policy.

`pilot-v124-day13-decision-timing/` repeats the same first twelve nightly rows byte-for-byte and stops at the same day-thirteen time 295 s. The opt-in timing observer recorded 295 one-second daylight decisions: 6,121 ms total in player-policy decisions and 16,060 ms in real `Game.tick` calls. The largest two decisions, at times 240 and 250 s, cost 2,782 and 2,521 ms; the maximum individual tick was 1,190 ms. This confirms that the multi-minute stall observed later in v122 cannot be attributed to this daylight interval. A timed exact continuation into the raid is still needed.

`pilot-v125-day13-raid-timing/` repeated the same twelve complete nightly rows exactly and continued to a diagnostic checkpoint at day-thirteen time 390 s. The trace has 318 steps. All decisions in the final five rows took at most 0.12 ms. Ticks beginning at times 365, 370, 375 and 380 s cost only 4–7 ms each with no raid. The **single 5-s native tick beginning at time 385 s took 320,197 ms** and ended with the active raid. Its paired decision took 0.04 ms. This is the dominant multi-minute stall. A no-player diagnostic from the earlier time-295 checkpoint had measured about 10.9 s for the nominally similar spawn tick, so navigator/entry context or intervening state makes a material difference. The timed native checkpoint at time 390 is retained; it is not a completed thirteenth night or a balance defeat. The next probe must attribute this exact tick to native navigation/entry subcalls before making a candidate optimization.

`pilot-v126-day13-spawn-trace/` repeats the twelve nightly rows exactly. The day-thirteen tick beginning at 385 s took 323,640 ms; its decision took 0.04 ms. Optional in-engine stages show that raid-entry preparation took 0.08 ms, selection completed at 2,540 ms, residency at 2,541 ms, and all sixteen animals were spawned at 2,543 ms. Thus entry selection and spawn account for less than 1% of the stall. The remaining time occurs in the same tick after spawn, during native raid or worker updates. The retained checkpoint at 390 s is a diagnostic stop, not a completed night. The next trace separates those phases and individual animal target searches; no economic, agricultural or military parameter has changed.

`pilot-v127-day13-update-phases/` repeats the twelve daily rows and the same stall (352,913 ms at time 385). Its phase log was accidentally filtered out because the substep `time` field overwrote the tick-start time. That instrumentation defect is corrected in source; the v127 receipt is retained as a negative diagnostic, not presented as a phase measurement. To validate the corrected trace cheaply, `day13-no-player-phase-trace.json` and `day13-no-player-target-detail.json` replay the copied time-295 state without player decisions. This replay is **not** a campaign. Its spawn tick cost 14–15 s. Raid updates consumed 13.5 s, worker updates 10 ms, and the sixteen target searches consumed 13.49 s. A detailed repeat measured 2,563 physical approach searches at 13.93 s versus 2,624 group-accessibility checks at 0.25 s. `day13-no-player-target-kind.json` confirms all sixteen eventually selected walls after checking approximately 158 crop approaches each. The full native campaign's 353-s phase split remains unverified, but the isolated replay gives a concrete route-search candidate to investigate. In the full campaign checkpoint, all sixteen animals retired with unused hits after `no-reachable-unleased-target`, so entry distance and physical navigation need examination before military calibration.

`tools/probe-compact-raid-entry.mjs` probes the retained time-390 formation using the same native entry selector, terrain, structures and sixteen recorded species, with a reconstructed center camera; it is a geometry diagnostic rather than a resumed campaign. Groups of 4, 8 and 12 selected exterior entrances at mean distances 27.8, 41.2 and 35.0 m from the center. A group of 16 selected the distant active-region edge at 133.8 m. An opt-in attempt to find a compact sixteen-animal formation around all sixteen directions failed native validation and was reverted. The finding motivates an experiment that keeps all sixteen physical animals and their hit budgets but assembles two non-overlapping, independently validated exterior subformations; it does not justify changing horde counts or damage yet.

The split-formation candidate now runs on this experimental branch. `day13-split-entry-probe-v3.json` found six separated pairs of eight with complete native exterior validation. The deterministic opposite-side search selected sixteen positions at a mean 42.3 m from the farm in 1.09 s on the retained geometry; birth and exit points of different squads are physically separated. The no-player copied-state replay `day13-no-player-split-entry.json` reduced the first raid tick from 10.7–15.3 s in earlier diagnostic runs to 7.66 s, and all sixteen animals held distinct wall targets rather than retiring immediately. These are **diagnostic** results, not a completed campaign or a balance approval. The next acceptance gate is an exact native-policy pilot through the same night and subsequent attack, followed by broader biome/seed tests. A failed near formation retains the prior exterior selector; no animal count, hit budget, damage, economy or player policy has changed.
