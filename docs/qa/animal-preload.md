# Preparing the five animal species before gameplay

Previously `warmAnimalModels()` downloaded only the expected species. Actual actor creation still cloned its skeleton, normalized every clip, gathered grounding vertices and created animation bindings on first appearance. GPU programs, textures, geometry and bone textures could also remain uninitialized until that first draw.

World loading now prepares all five original species before revealing gameplay. Each species retains shared normalized clips and one prepared skeleton/mixer. The first actor consumes that spare; later actors borrow the same geometry, materials and clips with independent skeletons and mixers. This is a bounded first-instance cache, not a pool growing with campaign history. Preparation binds all animation clips, initializes the running pose and updates skinned bounds.

Before loading completes, the production renderer asynchronously compiles the animal materials against the real scene lights, then performs a 16×16 offscreen draw with the rigs temporarily uncullable. This also uploads geometry, textures and bone textures. The staging group and render target are removed, previous rendering state is restored and shadow caches invalidated. Native models, heroic scales and animations are unchanged.

The [browser fixture](../../tests/browser/animal-preload.html) creates an ordinary paid center and spawns a real five-species raid in the native WorldScene. It focuses their actual spawn positions. Reports for [Grand Canyon](animal-preload/gran-canon.json), [Mangroves](animal-preload/manglares.json), [Savanna](animal-preload/sabana.json), [Volcanoes](animal-preload/volcanes.json), [Grand River](animal-preload/gran-rio.json) and [Desert](animal-preload/desierto.json) pass: all five rigs ready, seven GLB loads before and after, and no new animal material program IDs on appearance. The camera can introduce unrelated terrain programs, so the fixture records those separately instead of relying on equal global program counts.

These are single desktop-browser samples. They demonstrate removal of first-use model loading and animal material compilation from the tested appearance, not zero frame spikes, mobile FPS, cold network loading speed or all-biome acceptance. Loading the five species up front increases initial preparation and retained memory; that cost belongs behind the loading screen. Large groups can still need additional skeleton clones beyond the single prepared spare per species.

Three focused tests verify deduplicated concurrent loading, first-rig consumption, shared resources with independent mixers, retry after a failed download and cancellation during pending loading. The resource-lifetime case also verifies that a departing actor releases its private bone texture exactly once without disposing shared geometry/materials. The same cleanup applies to departing workers and canceled actor attachments. The affected animation, material registry and shadow suites pass together: 40 tests, zero failures. Production build and web-package checks also pass.
